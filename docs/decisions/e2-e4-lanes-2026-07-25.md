# Lane (a) — E2 (reference retrieval) and E4 (entity resolution): re-arm or retire?

**Decided 2026-07-25** · analysis + recommendation · owner call recorded at the end.

## Where the two lanes actually stand

Both were scored once against pre-registered floors on 2026-07-12, both missed, both wear
**"floors not met — experimental / advisory only"** labels, and both publish their full
scoreboards. Neither is dormant code pretending to be a feature: each shipped the *simpler*
thing its own exam vindicated.

**E2 — reference retrieval** (`evals/rag/`, `docs/e2-rag-preregistration.md`)
- Retrieval hit-rate **19/24 = 0.79** against a **≥0.85** floor. Missed.
- The embedding hybrid **failed to beat plain BM25**, so the simpler lane shipped.
- `goldExposed: true` — the split is burned. Re-scoring it would be meaningless.

**E4 — entity resolution** (`evals/entity/`, `docs/e4-entity-resolution-preregistration.md`)
- Hard **zero-false-merge** floor on near-miss traps: **14/14 held.**
- The fuzzy ensemble **tied** normalized-exact matching: precision 18/18, recall **18/35 =
  0.51** against a **≥0.80** floor. Missed.
- Exact matching — always the protected default — remains the shipped default.
- One demonstrated added value: routing **9/10 genuinely-ambiguous pairs to a human**
  instead of guessing.
- **A first scoring run was VOIDED** when cross-model review found its corpus carried 10
  near-miss traps against the registered ≥12 minimum. A fresh split was generated and
  re-scored, and the honest number came back **worse** (recall 0.6 → 0.55) — and was kept.

## Recommendation: **RETIRE E2's embedding hybrid. KEEP both lanes as they are. Re-arm neither.**

### Why not re-arm

Re-arming means: build a fresh registered split, commit new floors before running, spend an
owner-armed pass. That is the right cost when there is a **reason to expect a different
result.** There isn't one here.

- **E2** missed on *retrieval hit-rate over a fixed committed corpus* — a corpus of rule,
  schema, and glossary text that has not grown. Re-running the same method over the same
  material to get a better number is the thing pre-registration exists to prevent. A real
  re-arm needs a genuinely stronger method (better chunking, a reranker), which is a build,
  not a re-score. **That build is not currently worth it**: the lane is advisory-only, and
  nothing downstream depends on its hit-rate.
- **E4** is the more interesting case, because its floor miss is *informative rather than
  disappointing*: the fuzzy ensemble tied exact matching at n=35. That is a finding — at this
  corpus's scale and shape, fuzziness buys nothing over exact matching, and the honest
  default is the simple one. Re-arming to chase 0.80 recall would be optimizing a method the
  evidence says is not needed.

### Why not retire the lanes either

Retiring would delete the most credible evidence in the repo. **E4's VOID-and-re-score is
the single strongest anti-theater artifact the project has** — an independent audit this
session called it exactly that, over and above the classifier work. A lane that missed its
floor, published the miss, voided its own convenient first run, and kept a *worse* honest
number demonstrates the measurement discipline better than any passing number could. Deleting
it to tidy the status table would be trading the proof for the appearance of polish.

They also cost nothing to keep: both are offline, `$0`, deterministic, locked by
`*-results-lock.test.ts`, and green in every run.

### The one thing to actually retire

**E2's embedding-hybrid path.** It was tested, it lost to BM25, and it is the only part of
either lane carrying weight it did not earn — a model cache, a cache-gated test that skips in
CI, and an inference path that exists to support a conclusion already reached ("BM25 is
enough"). Retiring it removes the repo's only cache-gated skip and its only would-be
inference dependency, and the finding it produced survives in the record.

Deferred to a separate pass: it touches the CI skip-count claim in the README's status table,
so it needs its own red-green change rather than riding this one.

## What "keep as-is" commits to

1. The labels stay **"floors not met — experimental, advisory only"** in the README, SHOWCASE,
   and every payload. No softening.
2. Both scoreboards stay published, misses included.
3. **A re-attempt requires a fresh registered split and new pre-committed floors** — the
   burned splits are never re-scored. Already the stated rule; restated because "re-arm
   later" is exactly where that rule gets quietly dropped.
4. **Re-check trigger** (so this is not an undated "not yet" that ages into a permanent no):
   revisit if the reference corpus grows materially, if a downstream surface starts *depending*
   on retrieval quality rather than treating it as advisory, or if entity resolution is asked
   to run over a corpus large enough that exact matching visibly fails.

## Status

**No code change. Labels and scoreboards unchanged.** One deferred cleanup recorded (E2's
embedding hybrid). Both lanes remain as evidence of the measurement discipline, which is the
role they are currently best at.
