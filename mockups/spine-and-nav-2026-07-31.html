Design brief · sequence, not surface · 2026-07-31

The spine and the shelf.

Curbside Commons has five destinations presented as peers and an instrument that opens with two empty boxes. It has a sequence — it just isn’t shaped like one. This document designs that sequence and proposes nothing about the surface.

Fixed
Onest display + body · JetBrains Mono for data only · ultramarine #2438d6 as the single accent · light only · workbench-first · production register · desktop only · 1180px centred column.
Pinned
Nav labels and the five landing H2s are held by regression tests. Any move that touches them is an owner decision, and every such move below says so.
Out of scope
Visual refresh. Not one colour, weight, radius or face is proposed here.

The spine

Four beats. Every surface belongs to exactly one.

The visitor asks four questions in a fixed order. Today the site answers all four simultaneously and lets them choose. That is the whole defect.

Beat 1 · Show me

Make it do something

“What is this thing?”

The instrument demonstrates itself on the bundled pair before it asks for anything. One control, on screen, with a consequence the visitor can see.

hero · workbench

Beat 2 · Read it

The verdict is the destination

“What did it find?”

Findings with the arithmetic attached — and the provenance of the data sitting inside the verdict, not one route away from it.

wb-result · /report

Beat 3 · Trust it

Reachable from the claim

“Why should I believe it?”

The rule a finding cites, and the scoring behind the engine, are reached from that finding — not by returning to the bar and guessing.

/fees · /proof

Beat 4 · Do it

Now bring yours

“With my own data?”

The upload is the second offer. It arrives after the instrument has earned the commitment, which is the only order that makes sense.

wb-slots

Beats 2, 3 and 4 are largely built. Beat 1 is where the sequence breaks, and it breaks before the visitor has done anything — which is why it costs more than its size suggests.

The shelf

One verb, then a shelf that is in order.

The bar has one emphasized action as of tonight. What it still lacks is any signal that the other four are a sequence rather than a menu.

The beat labels above are annotation for this document — they do not ship. The shipping signal is the hairline and the order. Position is already carried by the live route readout, which states something true of every route; extending it to state position would change e2e-pinned text and is therefore an owner decision, not a design one.

Refused, and worth recording: the obvious way to show sequence in a nav is to number it. This site was de-numbered by owner word on 2026-07-28 — numerals told visitors it was a document to be read in order. Numbering the bar would reintroduce exactly the read that decision removed. Order is carried by position and grouping, never by digits.

The finding this document exists to surface

M1 and M2 are one move, not two. Giving the run control a live verb at first paint (M1) does nothing if the control is below the fold, because the visitor never sees it. Reclaiming the fold (M2) does little on its own, because what it reveals is a disabled button and a sentence explaining why it cannot be used.

M2 is owner decision 1, and it came back — not decided —. It was presented as a spacing question worth about 2px of argument. It is not: it is the precondition for the site’s first screen having a working verb. That reframing is the one thing here that should change how the decision gets made.

What this does not decide

Four owner calls, none of them taken here.

  • A The fold reclaim, reframed Not spacing polish — the precondition for beat 1. Same change, different question.
  • B Whether the shelf may be reordered The nav test pins labels; whether it pins order needs reading before anyone moves a link.
  • C The sentence at the verdict M4 adds copy to an honesty-gated surface. It must state a fact about the data, never caption the site as a demo — the distinction the production-register sweep already drew.
  • D The sixty-second sentence What should a visitor be able to say after a minute here? Every move above is justified against an answer that has not been written down yet.

Evidence status

Every claim carries a rung. This document is rung 1 — it was rendered and inspected at 1280×900. The site it describes is not: out/ was built at 15:15, before the Onest reversal, and still carries the retired “Example report” label — so rendering it would measure the previous design, not this one.

  • Rung 1 · rendered measurement — this document. Chromium launches under --single-process; the multi-process spawn was the blocker two sessions hit. Ports still refuse to bind (EPERM), so no dev server and no e2e run — but a page can be served from disk by request interception.
  • Rung 2 · pinned in CI — the fold contract, the H2 order, the nav contract. The seven assertions above are written to join this rung.
  • Rung 3 · read from source — the affordance order inside each slot and its measured type scale, canRun requiring only the feed, the provenance already present in the result panel. This is where the diagnosis sits.
  • Rung 4 · computed from the stylesheet — the fold arithmetic and the block heights in the comparison above. Indicative, not measured against a live build.
  • Rung 5 · judgment — the four beats, the ordering, and every word of the recommendation.
