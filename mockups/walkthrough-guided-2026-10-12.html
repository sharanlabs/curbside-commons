The menu customers order from is a copy. Copies drift.

A price, a modifier or a sold-out item changes in the merchant’s record and the platform’s copy doesn’t follow. People — and now AI agents — order from the copy, and the mistake comes back as a refund. This finds the drift, explains and sizes it, and drafts the message. A person signs before anything acts.

How a receipt is computed

Each claim in the copy is matched to the record row it should agree with, by the id-mapping the merchant’s operations team owns. The rule for that field is applied exactly — integer cents, no rounding, no clock, no model.

The same two files produce the same report, byte for byte. That is what lets a finding be argued about on its facts rather than on whether the tool was having a good day.

DEFER

Model-written explanation

The language-model seat that would add a written explanation has not passed its exam on this model. It stays off until it does.

The six causes the table can name
  • sync-latency — a sync is due and has not run yet.
  • merchant-edit — the record changed and nothing carried it over.
  • integrator-bug — a sync ran and wrote the wrong value.
  • platform-override — a portal edit replaced the integration’s value.
  • encoding — the same value, misread (cents as dollars).
  • unknown — the log does not say, and the output says so.

Every assumption in this arithmetic

Three of the four lines are counts and sums over the orders file and need no assumption. The fourth multiplies by a share that is declared, not measured, and the output names it as an assumption every time it is used.

On a platform’s own exports, inside its own environment, the same formulas run over its orders. This page never receives that data.
